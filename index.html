case study — redesign

Replacing a keg-based product line with a post-mix generation, and opening a market ten times larger.

In early 2025 I came on as engineering lead for a small partnership building commercial beverage dispensing hardware. The existing product line was keg-based — nitrogen tanks, pressure-fed tubing — and only worked in buildings of 250+ units.

I led the replacement: a post-mix architecture, with concentrate brewed and mixed at the point of dispense, in a smaller enclosure. I worked between the people who understood the deployment problem and the people who understood the manufacturing constraints.

The new line works at 50+ units, and it also fits sites that units don't measure at all — offices, banks, clinics. That is where most of the market came from.

First units are in the field and the design is production-ready.

case study — modular synthesis

Creating musical systems as richly interdependent as our modern world.

I was the firmware engineer on a quad envelope generator — four ADSR channels on a Microchip PIC32, where the channels modulate and trigger each other instead of simply running side by side. That cross-coupling is the whole design: it turns four ordinary envelopes into a system whose behavior you discover rather than specify, and the demos found gestures none of us had planned for. Hardware engineer and product engineer in the other two seats; SPI, I2C and UART throughout.
