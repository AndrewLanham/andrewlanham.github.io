Engineering Scientist · Quantum computing research

Center for Quantum Research, Applied Research Laboratories · The University of Texas at Austin

The group focuses on the quantum–classical boundary, navigating the interplay of quantum and classical processing for applications. That work involved designing quantum-inspired algorithms and evaluating proposed quantum techniques against their classical counterparts.

I worked primarily on statistical characterization and signal processing for hardware and algorithms. This included significant work in embedded and sensor design.

  • Space-time block code communication protocol — a classical, quantum-inspired multi-antenna protocol for highly resource-constrained environments, drawing upon the principles of quantum error correction. Patent awarded.
  • Resource state simulation for fusion-based quantum computing — a classical stochastic model that mimics the entangled resource states FBQC is built from, reaching 94.7% quantum fidelity on a four-star state and 70.6% on an eight-qubit GHZ state, at estimated generation rates in the hundreds of MHz.
  • Analog hardware for quantum systems evaluation — PCB design and embedded sensing.

Modular synthesizer component design

Bad Hair Modular

I collaborated with a small company on modular synthesizer component design. Our module features parallel envelope generation channels that allow for unique inter-channel modulation and triggering, with hundreds of unique configurations. The demos revealed new complexity in modular musical expression. I was the embedded engineer on the project, writing the firmware on a Microchip PIC32, working with a hardware engineer and a product engineer.

Embedded protocols throughout — SPI, I2C, UART.
